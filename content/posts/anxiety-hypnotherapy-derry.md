## Anxiety Is Not a Personality Trait

One of the most damaging beliefs many anxious people carry is that this is just who they are, that anxiety is woven into their character, a permanent feature of their inner landscape.

It isn't.

Anxiety is a learned response. Somewhere in your history, often in childhood or adolescence, sometimes in adulthood following a period of intense stress or trauma, your mind learned to respond to certain situations as though they were dangerous. That learning happened in the subconscious, below the level of rational thought, and that's exactly why reasoning yourself out of anxiety so rarely works.

You cannot think your way out of a pattern that isn't held in thought.

## Why Medication Alone Has Limits

Medication for anxiety (antidepressants, beta-blockers, benzodiazepines) can be genuinely helpful for many people, and there's no shame in using it. For some, it's essential. But medication manages symptoms. It does not, in itself, change the underlying pattern that generates the anxiety.

When the medication stops, the pattern remains.

Clinical hypnotherapy takes a different approach. Rather than suppressing the anxiety response, it works to understand and update the subconscious programme that is generating it, addressing the cause rather than muting the effect.

This doesn't mean hypnotherapy and medication are mutually exclusive. Many clients come to Tracey while continuing to use medication, and hypnotherapy works alongside it effectively. Some clients, in consultation with their GP, are able to reduce medication over time as the underlying anxiety patterns resolve.

## What Is Happening in the Anxious Brain

When the brain perceives a threat, real or imagined, it activates the amygdala, the region responsible for the fear response. The amygdala doesn't distinguish well between actual danger and perceived danger. It responds to a difficult phone call much as it would respond to a physical threat.

The result is a cascade of physiological changes: raised heart rate, shallow breathing, muscle tension, a narrowing of attention. The body is preparing to fight or flee. In a genuinely dangerous situation, this is lifesaving. In modern daily life, it is exhausting.

In people with chronic anxiety, this threat response has become too sensitive, triggering too easily, too intensely, and too often. The amygdala has essentially been misprogrammed to treat ordinary situations as emergencies.

## How Hypnotherapy Recalibrates the Response

In a hypnotic state, the analytical, critical part of the conscious mind relaxes. This allows the therapist to work directly with the subconscious patterns that are maintaining the anxiety response.

During sessions with Tracey, the work typically involves:

- **Identifying the original source** of the anxiety pattern: the experiences or beliefs that taught the subconscious mind to respond with fear
- **Updating those associations** so that the triggers no longer carry the same threat charge. The memory remains; the distress attached to it is reduced
- **Reprogramming the default response**: building in feelings of calm, control, and safety as the baseline, rather than alert and braced for danger
- **Teaching self-regulation techniques**: including self-hypnosis and breathing practices that clients can use independently between sessions and long after the course of therapy ends

The change can be subtle at first, a slight loosening of tension, situations feeling marginally less threatening. For others it is more immediate. Either way, the shift is cumulative.

## What Anxiety Conditions Does Hypnotherapy Help With?

Clinical hypnotherapy is effective for a wide range of anxiety presentations, including:

- **Generalised Anxiety Disorder (GAD)**: persistent, low-level worry that feels impossible to switch off
- **Social anxiety**: intense fear of judgment, embarrassment, or rejection in social situations
- **Health anxiety**: persistent worry about illness or physical symptoms
- **Performance anxiety**: in professional, academic, or personal contexts
- **Panic attacks**: sudden, intense surges of fear often without an obvious trigger
- **Phobias**: intense, irrational fear of specific objects or situations

If you're unsure whether your experience fits any of these categories, don't worry. Tracey works with people, not diagnoses.

## What the Research Shows

Hypnotherapy has a growing evidence base for anxiety treatment. [A 2016 study from Stanford University](https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2016/07/study-identifies-brain-areas-altered-during-hypnotic-trances.html) showed that people in a hypnotic state display measurable changes in brain activity, particularly in areas related to self-consciousness and focus, that help explain why the intervention is effective for anxiety and stress.

[Meta-analyses have consistently shown](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7673546/) that cognitive-behavioural therapy combined with hypnotherapy produces significantly better outcomes than CBT alone for anxiety disorders. The addition of hypnosis appears to accelerate and deepen the change.
