## Why This Comparison Matters

If you want to quit smoking, you have more options than ever. Nicotine replacement therapy in a dozen forms. Prescription medication. Apps. Support groups. E-cigarettes. Hypnotherapy. Acupuncture.

The problem is that almost every provider claims their approach works, and it can be genuinely difficult to know where to start or which is most likely to succeed for you personally.

This article is an honest look at the most common options, including their actual long-term success rates where the evidence exists, and where clinical hypnotherapy sits in that picture.

## Nicotine Replacement Therapy (Patches, Gum, Lozenges)

**How it works:** Nicotine replacement therapy (NRT) reduces withdrawal symptoms by supplying a controlled, decreasing dose of nicotine without the harmful chemicals in cigarette smoke. The theory is that if withdrawal is less uncomfortable, you are more likely to break the habit.

**What the evidence shows:** NRT roughly doubles the chances of quitting compared to going cold turkey. That sounds encouraging until you look at the baseline. Cold turkey has a long-term success rate of around 3-5%. NRT brings this up to roughly 6-10% at one year.

**The fundamental limitation:** NRT addresses physical nicotine dependence. It does nothing for the psychological habit: the deeply grooved associations between certain triggers (stress, boredom, social situations) and the urge to smoke. This is why most people who use NRT still struggle with cravings long after the physical withdrawal should have passed.

## Vaping / E-Cigarettes

**How it works:** E-cigarettes deliver nicotine through vapour rather than smoke, removing most of the combustion-related chemicals associated with smoking-related disease.

**What the evidence shows:** Vaping is more effective than NRT for helping people stop smoking combustible cigarettes. A 2019 New England Journal of Medicine study found that e-cigarettes led to abstinence from smoking at one year in about 18% of participants, compared to 9.9% for NRT.

**The fundamental limitation:** Most people who switch to vaping remain dependent on nicotine. They have changed the delivery mechanism, not escaped the addiction. Vaping is also not without health risks. The long-term effects are not yet fully understood, and many people find it difficult to stop vaping once they have started.

For many people, vaping is a harm reduction step, not a cessation strategy.

## Prescription Medication (Varenicline / Champix, Bupropion)

**How it works:** Varenicline (brand name Champix, now withdrawn in some markets due to contamination concerns) and bupropion are prescription medications that act on brain receptors associated with nicotine's reward effect, reducing cravings and the pleasure associated with smoking.

**What the evidence shows:** Varenicline had the highest clinical success rate of any pharmacological intervention, roughly 25-30% abstinence at one year in clinical trials. Bupropion performs somewhat less well.

**The fundamental limitation:** Medication addresses the neurochemical reward pathway. It does not address the habitual, psychological, or emotional dimensions of smoking. Side effects (including vivid dreams, nausea, and in some patients mood changes) are common. Access through the NHS has also become more limited.

## Hypnotherapy

**How it works:** Clinical hypnotherapy works on the subconscious mind, where the smoking habit actually lives. Rather than managing withdrawal or blocking reward pathways, it directly rewrites the associations between triggers and the urge to smoke, and instals a new non-smoker identity.

**What the evidence shows:** The evidence base for hypnotherapy and smoking cessation is more difficult to standardise than pharmaceutical trials, because hypnotherapy is not a uniform intervention. Quality, technique, and the therapeutic relationship vary significantly between practitioners.

That said, a 2012 systematic review in the International Journal of Clinical and Experimental Hypnosis found that hypnotherapy compared favourably with behavioural counselling and NRT when delivered by skilled practitioners. Individual studies report abstinence rates at 12 months ranging from 20% to over 40%.

A University of Iowa meta-analysis examining 633 studies on smoking cessation found hypnosis to be the single most effective cessation method, achieving an average success rate more than twice that of nicotine replacement alone.

**Why it often works when other methods fail:** The key difference is that hypnotherapy addresses the *identity* and *psychology* of smoking, not just the biochemistry. Clients leave feeling like non-smokers, not smokers under strain, which is a fundamentally different and more stable outcome.

## Which Method Is Right for You?

There is no single answer. Some factors worth considering:

- **If your primary challenge is physical withdrawal**, NRT or medication may help manage the transition period
- **If you smoke primarily in response to stress, emotion, or habit**, hypnotherapy addresses these directly in a way no pharmaceutical intervention can
- **If you have tried multiple methods without success**, this strongly suggests the issue is psychological rather than biochemical, which is exactly where hypnotherapy operates
- **If you want to stop completely, not switch to another form of nicotine**, hypnotherapy is the only mainstream approach that creates a clean break from nicotine entirely

It is also worth knowing that hypnotherapy and NRT are not mutually exclusive. Some clients use NRT to manage the physical transition while hypnotherapy addresses the psychological habit simultaneously.

## The Question Nobody Asks

Most people who ask "which method should I use?" are really asking: "which method requires the least from me?"

The honest answer is that quitting smoking requires something from you regardless of method. The real question is *what* it requires.

NRT and medication require you to manage discomfort and side effects over time.

Hypnotherapy requires something different: genuine readiness to change, and willingness to engage with a process that works from the inside out.

If that readiness is present, the results tend to be both faster and more permanent than anything that works from the outside in.
