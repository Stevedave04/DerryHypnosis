The flight is booked. It is six weeks away. And you have already started doing the thing where you check the weather for that date, and look up the aircraft type, and read one more article about turbulence that you know will not help.

For a lot of people from the North West, the hardest part of Christmas is not the shopping or the in-laws. It is a short flight they would rather not be on.

## Key Takeaways

- Fear of flying is a specific phobia, not a character flaw, and it is one of the most treatable things a hypnotherapist sees.
- It usually takes between one and three sessions, which makes it unusually practical to address before a booked flight.
- The fear is rarely about the aircraft. It is more often about enclosure, loss of control, or not being able to leave.
- Start a few weeks before you fly, not the night before.

## It Is Not Really About Aviation

People who are frightened of flying tend to know more about aviation safety than people who are not. They have read the statistics. They know about redundant systems. None of it has made any difference, which is usually taken as proof that the fear is irrational and unfixable.

It is neither. It is that the information is landing in the wrong part of the mind.

When you look closely, the fear is usually one of these, and which one it is matters:

**Enclosure.** The door closes and you cannot get off. For many people this is the whole thing, and it is closer to claustrophobia than to a fear of crashing. These are the people who are fine once the doors open at the other end, and who find the taxiing worse than the flight.

**Loss of control.** You have handed your safety to someone you have never met. People who are comfortable driving in dreadful conditions are often terrified as a passenger in perfect ones — the difference is not the risk, it is the steering wheel.

**Panic about panicking.** The fear is not of the plane but of having a panic attack in a sealed tube in front of strangers with no exit. The anticipation generates the very arousal that is feared.

**A specific trigger.** Turbulence, take-off, the sounds the aircraft makes as the gear retracts.

**Something that happened.** A bad flight, a frightening landing, or sometimes simply being on board the week something was in the news.

Work on "flying" in the abstract and you get nowhere. Work on the actual mechanism and it tends to move quickly.

## Why This One Responds Well

Specific phobias are among the things clinical hypnotherapy handles most reliably, and fear of flying is a particularly clean example. The trigger is well defined, the response is predictable, and there is a clear before and after.

Most people need one to three sessions. Compared with work on anxiety or eating patterns, which takes longer because it is woven into daily life, a phobia is a contained problem.

The work generally covers rewiring the automatic response to the specific trigger, reducing the anticipatory build-up in the weeks beforehand, and giving you something concrete to use in the seat — a breathing pattern or anchor you have actually practised, rather than advice you read once.

What it does not do is make you enjoy flying. The honest aim is that you get on the plane, get through it, and get off at the other end without the fortnight of dread beforehand.

## Flying Home from the North West

A practical note for anyone here. Plenty of people in Derry and Donegal fly from City of Derry, Belfast International, Belfast City or Dublin, and people coming home for Christmas are often arriving from London, Manchester, Glasgow or further.

That means three things worth planning around:

**Your flight may be short.** A hop to Glasgow or Manchester is not long in the air. People sometimes assume a short flight is easier; for those whose fear is about enclosure and not being able to leave, duration is not really the variable.

**December weather means turbulence.** Winter flying over this part of the world is bumpier than summer flying. Knowing that in advance, and understanding that turbulence is uncomfortable rather than dangerous, takes some of the surprise out of it.

**The journey is often two flights or a long drive first.** If you are routing through Dublin, the day is longer and the tiredness itself makes anxiety harder to manage. Build that into the plan.

## When to Start

The most common mistake is leaving it until the week of the flight.

If you are flying on the 20th of December, starting in late October or early November gives room for two or three sessions plus practice between them. It also means you spend those weeks working on it rather than dreading it, which is a materially different six weeks of your life.

Short notice is still worth doing — something is better than white-knuckling it — but more time gives a better result.

If you are not flying this Christmas but avoided booking because of this, that is arguably the stronger reason to do something. The cost of a flying phobia is rarely one bad flight. It is the holidays not taken, the wedding abroad missed, the job that involved travel and went to someone else.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many sessions does it take to treat a fear of flying?

Typically one to three. Specific phobias are contained problems with a well-defined trigger, which is why they usually respond faster than generalised anxiety. Your situation is discussed properly at the free consultation.

### Can you help if my flight is next week?

Often, yes, though more notice gives a better result. Short-notice work tends to focus on the most useful pieces: reducing the anticipatory build-up and giving you something practical to use on the day. Mention the date when you get in touch.

### Is fear of flying really a phobia, or am I being dramatic?

It is a recognised specific phobia. It is also extremely common, and people who have it are often well informed about aviation safety, which tells you the problem is not a lack of information.

### What if my fear is really about being trapped rather than crashing?

That is very common, and identifying it matters, because the work is different. Enclosure-based fear overlaps with claustrophobia and is addressed differently from a fear focused on the aircraft itself.

### Will I need to fly as part of the treatment?

No. The work is done in the clinic or online, with something to practise between sessions. The flight itself is the test, not the homework.

### Can I have sessions online if I am abroad or travelling?

Yes. Sessions run by secure video as well as in person, which suits people working away or living outside the North West who are flying home.
